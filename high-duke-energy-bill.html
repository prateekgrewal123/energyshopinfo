Why Is My Duke Energy Bill So High?

A step-by-step checklist for investigating a higher electricity bill and identifying the most likely causes.

Home / Guide

1. Compare kWh usage

Start with consumption rather than the dollar amount. If kWh increased substantially, look for changes in HVAC use, weather, occupancy, appliances or daily routines.

2. Consider weather

Hot weather can increase air-conditioning demand; cold weather can increase heating demand. In 2026, Duke Energy specifically said extreme summer heat in North Carolina was contributing to higher cooling demand and bills for many customers.

3. Check the billing period

Count the days between the start and end dates. A longer cycle can produce a larger total.

4. Review household changes

  • New or frequently used appliances
  • More people at home
  • Lower or higher thermostat settings
  • More laundry or cooking
  • Pool pumps or other equipment

5. Review charges and adjustments

Look for previous balances, credits, taxes, fees, rate-related changes or other line items. A high total is not always caused by a single appliance.

6. Use usage tools

Where available, usage alerts, account dashboards and other provider tools can help customers see consumption during the billing cycle. Duke Energy currently highlights Bill Insights, Usage Alerts and Home Energy Reports as tools for understanding usage and bills.

When to contact the provider

If the usage and bill details do not explain the increase, contact the official provider and have the statement available. Ask for an explanation of the specific line items rather than providing sensitive credentials to a third party.
