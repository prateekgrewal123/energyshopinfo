Why Is My Duke Energy Bill So High? 8 Things to Check

A troubleshooting guide for an electricity bill that is higher than expected.

Independent informational article · Updated 2026

Home / Blog / Article

Compare kWh

Start by comparing kWh rather than only the dollar total. A large usage change points toward household consumption.

Check billing days

Count the days covered by both statements. A longer billing period can increase the total.

Review HVAC

Heating and cooling can operate for many hours and may be a major contributor during extreme temperatures.

Look for new equipment

New equipment such as portable cooling, space heaters, freezers or pool equipment can change consumption.

Consider household routines

Guests, working from home and changes in daily routines can raise electricity use.

Inspect charges

If kWh is similar, inspect individual charges, credits, taxes, fees and adjustments.

Compare several months

One month can be unusual. Compare several statements to identify a pattern.

Ask for clarification

If the figures remain unclear, provide the provider with your comparison notes.

Quick Question

What is the most useful first step? Start with the current statement and compare the specific figures relevant to the topic rather than relying on assumptions.
