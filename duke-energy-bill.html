Understanding Your Duke Energy Bill

Learn what common sections of an electricity bill mean, how to compare usage and what to check when the total changes.

Home / Guide

Start with the billing period

Look at the beginning and ending dates shown on the bill. A billing period that is longer or shorter than the previous period can affect the total amount due even when your daily habits have not changed.

Tip: When comparing two bills, compare both the total dollars and the number of days in each billing period.

Understand kWh usage

Electricity consumption is commonly expressed in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Your bill may show current usage alongside prior usage or historical comparisons. A larger kWh figure generally means the home used more electricity during that period.

  • Compare current kWh with the previous bill.
  • Look for seasonal patterns.
  • Consider weather and HVAC operation.
  • Check whether a new appliance or household change affected consumption.

Review charges and balances

Depending on the service area and bill format, you may see energy-related charges, delivery or distribution charges, taxes, fees, credits, adjustments, previous balances and payments. Read the bill line by line instead of assuming every change comes from electricity usage alone.

Check the due date

The due date tells you when the amount shown is expected. If you anticipate difficulty paying on time, review available options early rather than waiting until after the account becomes past due.

If something looks wrong

Gather the bill, compare it with prior statements and review your usage. If the discrepancy remains unexplained, contact the official provider through a verified channel. Do not send account credentials to an informational website.

Quick bill-review checklist

  • Billing start and end dates
  • Current kWh usage
  • Previous balance
  • Payments and credits
  • Current charges, taxes and adjustments
  • Amount due and due date
